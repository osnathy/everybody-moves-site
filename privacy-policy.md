# Privacy policy

Last updated 24 October 2026.

Everybody Moves is made by Osnat Haj Yahia, and I am responsible for this
policy. You can reach me at osnathy83@gmail.com.

The game doesn't ask for your name, your email or an account, and I don't run a
server that stores anything about you. The game shows ads from Google, and
Google collects some data to show them. This page says what that data is and
how to limit it.

## What stays on your phone

The game keeps your progress on your phone. That is which boards you've
finished, the fewest moves you've finished each one in and the swipes of that
run, whether your first finish of each board was in the fewest moves, which
boards you picked up a candy on, and a few things the game has already shown
you once, so it doesn't show them again. Your stars aren't stored separately.
The game works them out from that progress. It also keeps your settings for
sound, vibration and motion.

The game doesn't use your microphone, camera, contacts, photos or location
services.

Deleting the app deletes it. On iPhone, an iCloud backup can include it, and
reinstalling from that backup brings it back.

## Ads

The first three boards have no ads. From board 4 the game shows a banner below
the board. From the second chapter on it also shows, every so often, a
full-screen ad between two boards, never in the first chapter. The ads are
served by Google AdMob.

To choose and count the ads, Google's ad software collects:

- identifiers for your device. On iPhone that is always an identifier Apple
  gives each app maker for their own apps, and the advertising identifier only
  if you allow tracking. On Android it is the Android advertising ID and the
  app set ID.
- your internet address, which gives your approximate location
- which ads you see and tap, and when the app is opened
- crash reports and performance data from the ad software itself

Google's consent software also uses your internet address to work out which
privacy rules apply where you are, so it knows whether to ask you anything.

Google uses this data for advertising, for measuring ads and to prevent fraud.
It is sent to Google encrypted. Google keeps it under its own policy, which
explains how Google uses data from apps that show its ads:
[policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites](https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites).

On iPhone the game asks whether it may track you once, when you finish board 3.
Until you say yes, the ads you see aren't chosen using your activity in other
apps and websites, and if you say no they never are. You can change the answer
at any time in Settings, Privacy & Security, Tracking.

If you live in the European Economic Area, the UK or Switzerland, Google's
consent form asks for your choices before any ad is shown. If you live in a US
state with its own privacy law, you can opt out of ads chosen using your data.
Where Google's rules give you these choices, the game's Settings has an Ad
privacy row where you can change them later.

On Android you can reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's
settings, under Privacy, Ads.

The ads are limited to ones Google rates as suitable for all ages.

## Testing builds

The version from the App Store or Google Play keeps no record of how you play
beyond your progress.

Builds I give testers also keep a record of how each board went: how long it
took, your swipes, undos and restarts, whether you asked for a hint, and
whether an ad followed. It stays on the phone. A tester can send it to me with
the Share capture button in Settings, and chooses where it goes. The file has
no name, account, phone id or advertising id in it.

## Reporting an ad

Settings has a Report an ad row. It opens an email to me in your mail app, with
the subject filled in and nothing else about you. Nothing is sent unless you
send it.

## Game updates

The game gets its updates through Expo, the service it's built with. When it
checks for one, it tells Expo which version you have and whether you're on
iPhone or Android. It also sends a random code the game makes the first time
you open it, so Expo can recognise the same copy of the game next time.

That code isn't linked to your name, your phone number or anything else about
your phone, and I don't use it to track you. Expo's privacy policy is at
[expo.dev/privacy](https://expo.dev/privacy).

## Children

The game is meant for people aged 13 and over. It isn't directed at children
under 13, and I don't knowingly collect personal information from them. If you
think a child under 13 has been using the game, email me and I'll tell you how
to remove what the ads have collected.

## Why the data is used

Where the law asks for a reason, these are mine. Ads chosen using your data
rely on your consent, which you can withdraw in Settings, Ad privacy. You don't
have to give it, and the game plays the same if you don't. Showing
ads that are not personalised, preventing ad fraud, checking for updates and
answering your emails rely on my legitimate interest in keeping a free game
working.

## Where the data goes and how long it is kept

Google and Expo process data in the United States and in other countries. They
do it under their own terms, which protect it at least as well as this page
and use the safeguards the law requires for transfers, such as standard
contractual clauses. Google is certified under the EU-US Data Privacy
Framework. I'm in Israel, which the EU and the UK recognise as giving adequate
protection.

What the game keeps on your phone stays until you delete the app. Google and
Expo keep what they collect for as long as their own policies say. I keep
nothing about you, except emails you send me, which I delete within 12 months
of the last reply.

## If you live in the United States

In the last 12 months the game's ad software collected identifiers, internet
activity such as which ads you saw, and approximate location, for the purposes
above. Google's use of them for personalised ads may count as "sharing" under
California law. You can opt out in Settings, Ad privacy, where your state's law
gives you that choice, and on iPhone by not allowing tracking. I don't sell
personal information for money, and I don't knowingly sell or share the
information of anyone under 16.

## Your choices and your rights

Everything the game keeps is on your phone, and you can delete it by deleting
the app. I hold no copy, so there is nothing of yours on my side to hand over
or delete.

For the ad data Google holds, use the tracking and ad settings above, or
Google's own controls at [myadcenter.google.com](https://myadcenter.google.com).

Depending on where you live, you have the right to see the data held about
you, to have it corrected or deleted, to limit or object to its use, to take a
copy, and to withdraw your consent at any time. Email me and I'll help you do
that with Google. You can also complain to the data protection authority where
you live. In the UK that is the ICO, and in Israel the Privacy Protection
Authority.
